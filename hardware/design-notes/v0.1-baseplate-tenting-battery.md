# v0.1 Modular Baseplate — Tenting + 18650 Battery Bay

Status: **v0.1 conceptual + skeleton parametric CAD.** Iterative starting
point, not a fabrication-ready design. Companion OpenSCAD sources live in
[../cad/v0.1/](../cad/v0.1/); read that folder's README for render
instructions.

## Scope of this pass

Only the bottom baseplate is redesigned. The existing CASE, HOLDER, and
PLATE STLs in `hardware/cad/` are unmodified reference geometry — this
design must bolt on beneath them without altering the key/matrix layout,
thumb clusters, trackball, encoder, or either bowl curvature. ZMK firmware
config is also unmodified in this pass.

## What was actually inspected vs. assumed

The task gave bounding-box dimensions for all 6 existing meshes. I re-derived
those and cross-checked them directly against the STL files (numpy-stl); the
numbers match. I additionally inspected each file's own internal Z cross-
sections to check for structural features:

- **Reliable (measured, single-file, coordinate-frame-independent):** overall
  footprint sizes, plate thickness (3.0mm), holder envelope
  (82.5 × 45.2 × 8.5mm), and the fact that PLATE LEFT/RIGHT each have a
  full-footprint flat top face (the case-mating surface) with a smaller
  inset recess on the underside — i.e. the top face is the constraint that
  matters for case fit, and the underside is free to be reshaped.
- **Not reliable / not attempted:** exact case screw-boss / fastener hole
  positions, and the exact trackball/encoder cutout envelope on the right
  half. **Each exported STL is in its own local coordinate frame with no
  confirmed shared assembly origin**, so I could not trust cross-file Z
  offsets (e.g. "gap" between PLATE top and CASE bottom) as real stack-up
  measurements. I did not attempt hole-position mesh forensics (detecting
  cylindrical bores from raw triangle soup) in this pass — it's a fragile,
  time-consuming heuristic without a CAD kernel, and guessing wrong here is
  worse than flagging it. **This is the single biggest open item before any
  part of this design can be trusted for fabrication.**

## Mechanism 1: Modular tenting via swappable corner shims

Rather than a single continuous angled wedge slab (which would require a
matching angled mating face between case, wedge, and baseplate — fragile to
model and to reprint per angle), v0.1 uses **discrete-height corner
standoffs**:

- The new baseplate tray (`baseplate_tray.scad`) has a flat, **horizontal**
  top rim with 4 corner mounting posts, identical for every tenting angle.
- A separate, small **tenting shim set** (`tenting_shim_set.scad`) — 4 simple
  flat-top/flat-bottom blocks, no angled printed surfaces at all — sits
  between the tray rim and the (unmodified) case bottom rim. Two blocks (one
  lateral side, front + back) are a fixed "low" height; the other two
  (opposite lateral side) are taller by
  $\Delta h = W_{eff} \cdot \tan(\theta)$, where $W_{eff}$ is the lateral
  span between corner posts and $\theta$ is the desired tent angle.
- Changing the tenting angle = printing a new 4-block set (a few minutes,
  ~20×20mm blocks) and swapping the bolts — the tray and the case are never
  touched.
- This only implements single-axis **roll** tenting (raising one lateral
  side relative to the other), not front/back pitch. That matches "reduce
  forearm pronation," which is a roll-axis correction.
- Height math and per-angle values are computed and `echo()`-printed by
  `tenting_shim_set.scad` for whichever `TENT_ANGLE_DEG` you render.

Because both mating faces at every shim (top of tray, bottom of case) are
flat and horizontal/parallel, no sloped surface ever has to be matched
between two separately-printed parts — only a height (Z) value changes. This
is the lowest-risk way to get adjustable tenting without a CAD kernel to
verify complex angled-mate geometry.

## Mechanism 2: 18650 battery bay

- The tray is a full open-top box (not a thin 3mm plate like v1) — depth is
  a new parameter (`TRAY_DEPTH`, currently 16mm placeholder) sized to clear
  a protected flat-top 18650 cell inside a generic wire-lead holder
  (envelope ~21.5 × 76 × 12mm) plus a socketed nice!nano v2.
- Per the "keyed battery holder" requirement, this design does **not**
  reinvent battery spring contacts — it provides 2 printed mounting bosses
  to bolt down a purchased single-cell holder (which already provides
  polarity keying and retention), wired out via its own leads/JST pigtail to
  the controller and the switch/fuse.
- The cell/holder sits along the back edge of the tray in both halves; the
  nice!nano v2 shelf (4 small standoffs sized to its 18×33mm footprint) sits
  in a front corner, away from the battery zone.
- A panel cutout on the front wall reserves space for a slide/latch power
  switch; a small pass-through slot on the back rim reserves space for the
  flex interconnect running back up to the existing matrix PCB/holder area.
  Neither is dimensioned against a real switch part or real PCB connector
  position yet — both are placeholders (see open questions).

## Why the baseplate needs to hold the controller at all

The mode's own constraints note the existing HOLDER was sized for the
Seeed XIAO BLE footprint and is **not** mechanically compatible with a
nice!nano v2 (different shape/pinout). Since the HOLDER geometry is frozen
in this pass, the nice!nano v2 has nowhere else to physically live — the new
baseplate tray is the only place left to mount it, which is why the tray
carries a controller shelf in addition to the battery bay.

## Right half: trackball/encoder keepout

The right tray reserves a translucent, clearly-labeled placeholder keepout
volume (visible only in the `%` preview, not subtracted from the real solid)
near a guessed corner, and deliberately keeps the controller/battery layout
away from it. **This position is a guess, not a measurement** — the existing
CASE RIGHT / HOLDER RIGHT meshes were not analyzed for the actual trackball
sensor / encoder shaft protrusion depth or footprint in this pass. Do not
trust the right-side component layout until this is replaced with a real
value.

## Open questions / assumptions requiring your confirmation

1. **Tenting angle range & step.** Task text says 8–20°; `TENT_ANGLES` in
   `params.scad` currently seeds `[8, 12, 16, 20]` (4° step) as a guess.
   Confirm the actual range/step you want, and whether both halves should
   always tent to the same angle or independently.
2. **Case fastener positions.** `CORNER_INSET = [12, 12]` mm is a placeholder,
   not derived from the real case screw bosses. Needs either (a) physical
   measurement of the existing CASE/PLATE screw holes, or (b) a dedicated
   mesh-forensics pass to extract real hole coordinates, before the corner
   posts/shims can be trusted to align with the actual case.
3. **Trackball/encoder keepout (right half).** Real position/size of the
   PMW3610 + encoder protrusion below the case bottom rim is unknown from
   the meshes inspected so far. High priority to resolve before finalizing
   the right tray's internal layout.
4. **18650 cell + holder part selection.** "Flat-top protected 18650" was
   given as 18×65mm in the task, but real protected flat-top cells commonly
   run 67–70mm; `params.scad` uses 70mm generic envelope. Please confirm/pick
   an actual cell + holder SKU (dimensions + mounting-hole pattern) so the
   bay can be sized exactly instead of generously.
5. **Power switch part.** Cutout in `baseplate_tray.scad` (10×4mm slot) is a
   placeholder, not sized to a specific switch. Need a part number/datasheet.
6. **PTC resettable fuse.** Not modeled with real footprint yet — need a
   part choice (leaded radial vs. SMD-on-a-tab) before adding real mounting
   features.
7. **Battery orientation & serviceability.** Current layout assumes top-
   loading (remove case + shims to access the battery bay from above) rather
   than a side hatch. Confirm this is acceptable, or whether you want a
   separate bottom/side access hatch independent of removing the case.
8. **Stack height budget.** Because the existing STLs don't share a
   confirmed common origin, I don't have a trustworthy number for how much
   Z-height is actually available between the HOLDER underside and the
   original CASE bottom rim. Needed to confirm `TRAY_DEPTH` (16mm placeholder)
   doesn't need to grow further, and to confirm final overall keyboard height
   with tenting engaged is acceptable to you.
9. **Corner rounding / cosmetic match to case.** v0.1 trays use square outer
   corners for modeling simplicity/robustness; the real CASE likely has
   rounded corners. Cosmetic-only, deferred to a later pass once the
   mechanical layout is confirmed.

## Explicit CAD-authoring limitation

I authored `hardware/cad/v0.1/*.scad` as real parametric OpenSCAD source
(CSG solids: cubes, cylinders, boolean difference/union) — not a placeholder
or fake STL. However, **no OpenSCAD installation was available in this
environment**, so none of it has actually been rendered, checked for a valid
manifold solid, or exported to STL. Before printing anything: open each file
in OpenSCAD, confirm F6 "Render" succeeds without CGAL/manifold warnings, and
re-verify dimensions against whatever real parts you select for items 2–6
above.
